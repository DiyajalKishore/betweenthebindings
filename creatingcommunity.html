creating community — how queer spaces save lives

GALA Queer Archive creates welcoming communities for queer youth, a third space for them
to exist as themselves, unashamedly, creating a semblance of home.

The GALA Queer Archive held its first youth forum engagement meeting at the 7th floor of the Wits Art Museum, on Friday, April 24, inviting young queer individuals in and around the area to create connections and find a safe space— a home away from home.

Shared stories from attendees makes one realise that in a world where being ‘different’ is claimed to be accepted, one thing is clear: queerness is merely tolerated and never celebrated, especially in unqueer spaces. GALA does the opposite of this, fostering community and creating intentional spaces that celebrates and never tolerates.

Sign on display at GALA Queer Archive offices on the 7th floor at Wits Art Museum. Photo by: Kerese Govender.

The organisation is a non-profit archive, based at Wits Art Museum, but they operate independently and serve the public. Not a Witsie? That is completely fine, you are still welcome. The GALA Youth Forum is the place to visit; this is an open space for queer students to build connections and interact with each other.

Ikageng Olifant, a youth coordinator at GALA, hopes that the forum continues to foster safety for queer individuals and those still figuring out their sexuality. “I hope they have us in their becoming stories—GALA has a great way of tapping into that,” they added.

GALA is a home to every single member of the community. That is one answer that did not falter when attendees were asked, “What does GALA mean to you?”

“It is a space where I have always been able to ground myself,” said Onkokame Seeparmore, a youth coordinator for GALA.

As the sense of acceptance lessens and varies in volume, queerness is not just a title, not just a concept meant to rival beliefs, but rather an identity. It is a way of life, something that cannot simply be erased, regardless of how much effort has been poured into doing exactly that.

This organisation offers a very intimate experience of community for all those who wish to join. The meetings are held at the GALA library and begins with quick introductions and formalities before turning into a social gathering for the attendees. Queer youth are given the opportunity to be so unashamedly themselves around likeminded people.

“GALA is the place where I found what family means, what community means, the people that I have met here have really saved my life. It is home. It is my family. I wish everyone could experience a space like this in their lifetime,” Tlhatlogelo Somo said, a GALA Youth Forum regular attendee.

GALA has always existed as a means to “collect, preserve and share histories and narratives of LGBTQIA+ lives,” explained Kgomotso Kgasi, the Education Programmes Coordinator at GALA.

Thus, as it has been existing, shall it continue its mission to uplift, safely foster, save lives, and provide an intimate community to queer youth.
