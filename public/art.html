Who gets to dictate what art should be?

Everyone has a different opinion on what art is, but who decides which art is acceptable?

Art is all around us. It is the design on your morning chai latte. It is the spiral of the stairs in your office. It is the soundtrack to your car ride to school. It is the graffiti you see in the streets of Braamfontein. Yes, even the vulgar language spray painted on the walls of campus. Art is everywhere for those with eyes willing to see it. 

Who is to say what makes art, art? The saying goes, “Beauty is in the eye of the beholder.” Something beautiful in my eyes may not be to the next person.

The lines between self-expression and vandalism are often blurred in art forms such as graffiti. The opinions of what it actually is, tend to vary. Any creative would consider it a masterpiece. However, any usual, ‘9 to 5 tech bro’ would deem it a nuisance. 

The truth is, who gets to dictate whether it is prim and proper? No one. Beauty is subjective, and so is art. 

In this political climate, graffiti is used as an art medium, a way of communicating a message that no one seems to want to receive. It is the “Free Palestine” one sees on the stop sign in their office parking lot. It is the “Eat the Rich” one sees on a McDonald’s billboard on the highway. It is the “Say No to Violence” littered across stickers pasted on public bathroom walls. 

Graffiti at Wits University.
Photo by: Kerese Govender

Art is not supposed to be something that is neat and tidy, wrapped in a pink bow. Art is meaningful. It is supposed to stay with you. To linger in the silence that lurks after you have scrolled away from the news video about Congo. 

Even when art is filthy and vulgar, it is still art. This also includes the “Fuck this shit” on the side of a building. Art is all around us: even when we do not want to notice it.

When it really comes down to it, creatives make the world go round. From your favourite rapper to the actress you cannot help but fall in love with, even the barista at your go-to coffee shop. Of course, we have to remember the unknown graffiti tagger whose art you scrutinise. 

These are factors to consider when thinking about what makes art.

Art is everywhere and it belongs to everyone. The professional painters. The wannabe Soundcloud rappers. The amateur actor on TikTok. The poet on Substack. Yes, even the person with spray paint behind an abandoned building.
